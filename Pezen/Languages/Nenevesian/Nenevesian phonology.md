## Notes
- /d͡ʒ/ is lenited to \[ʒ] if the following vowel is not front /i(ʳ) e(ʳ)/ or long /ɛj ɔw a/.
- /p/ is lenited to \[f] if the following phoneme is a consonant or hiatus.
- \[ɹ] is the coda allophone of /d/, and may be a fricative \[ð].
- \[q] is the allophone of /k/ that occurs before purred vowels, /r/, and back vowels. \[ɴ χ] play similar roles for /ŋ x/, but these only consistently occur before purred vowels.
- /l/ is velarized \[ɫ] in some dialects, to distinguish it from /ʎ/.

## Chart

| Close-mid | -   | e eʳ  |         | o oʳ  |
| Open-mid  | -   |  ɛi   |    ə    | ɔu ɔʳ |
| Open      | -   |  ai   |    a    |  ɒu   |
## Notes
- The "purred vowels" (notated with a superscript trill, /ʳ/) originate from Middle Nenta /Vʁ/; either these are phonemic, or /ʁ/ is.
    - Purred vowels are also phonetically long, but are not considered "long vowels", as they do not consistently result from historic long vowels.
    - Several of these differ slightly compared to their plain vowel: /uʁ oʁ aʁ əʁ/ \[oʳ ɔʳ ɔʳ ɨʳ].
- Vowels before a coda /ɲ ŋ/ nasalize; this may also result in the nasals leniting to \[j̃ ɰ̃].
- The mid-rising diphthongs /ɛi ɔu/ may also flatten to \[eː oː]. Likewise, /a/ is the long version of /ə/; these three make up Nenevesian's true long vowels.
- When unstressed, /ai ɒu/ often "miss their mark", being pronounced somewhere near \[ae̯ ɒo̯]. This is shared with [[Matelian]] and northern [[Rokadong]] dialects, and therefore is an areal feature.
