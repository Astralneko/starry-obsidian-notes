## Notes

- Nasal consonants lose their contrast in the coda, with all turning into nasalization. However, they still contrast in initial position.
- /s, z, l, k, g/ palatalize before /i, j/, becoming [ɕ ʑ ʎ c ɟ]. These are typically pronounced identically to the actual palatal consonants, which means anything because...
- /c ɟ/ are often affricate-like [tɕ ɟʝ]. Note that these are not related to Classical Nenta /c ɟ/, as those became /t d/ in Kairitelan.
- /j w/ are highly limited in native words, as they only are not merged with another consonant (usually /ɟ g/ respectively) when after /a/.
- While /r/ is prototypically a trill, it usually becomes an approximant [ɹ] either in the coda or when adjacent to [ɯ ɐ] (but not [ɐ̃]).
- /l/ is often velarized for dissimilation from /ʎ/. However, this does not necessarily happen in all circumstances; different dialects velarize /l/ in different positions. Most commonly, /l/ is velarized when adjacent to /k g ɯ/.

## Notes

- Unstressed short /a/ is typically realized as [ɐ].
- [ɛ: ɔ:] are considered long forms of [e o], and are written accordingly.
- The schwar [ɹ̩], also written as [ɿ] to avoid diacritic spam, is phonemically equivalent to /ɯr./ where the /r/ is in the coda.
    - This is not to be confused with /ɯ.r/ where the /r/ isn't in the coda, as that is typically [ɯɹ].
    - In some dialects, further sound changes can result in a phonemic schwar; this almost always uses the Sinologist symbol.
    - Similarly, the sequences /ɯl ɯj ɯw/ typically are pronounced [ɘɫ ɘʑ~ɘʝ ɘɣ~ɘg].
- Nasalized vowels are phonemically equivalent to /VnC/, and are bimoraic when considering pitch contour, but the analysis as nasalized vowels is preferred.

# Stress and pitch

Kairitelan has two types of pitch accent: summit tone, where the accented syllable has high tone, and valley tone, where the accented syllable has low tone. The following mora brings the pitch back to neutral, which is what all other syllables have.

The accented syllable of a word is usually that which contains the penultimate mora. A mora is acquired for every vowel and for every stop or fricative coda. Like in [[Rokadong]], monosyllabic affixes usually do not change the accent. Unlike Rokadong, /r/ is not considered a stop. Together, this means that _lukar_ and _lukaran_ have the same accent.
