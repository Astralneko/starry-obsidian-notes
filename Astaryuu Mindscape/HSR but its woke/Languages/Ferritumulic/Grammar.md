## Word order

Despite its marking systems sometimes allowing for relatively free word order, Ferritumulic is a generally head-initial SVO language, the norm for indicative sentences in Pisco-Imerchali.

## Articles

The definite articles of Ferritumulic are very similar to Italian, with the series being _il, la, lhó, le_ for the masculine singular, feminine singular, masculine plural, and feminine plural, and with _il_ becoming _lu_ when the word it applies to starts with either two consonants or a palatal consonant. Since the sequence /ll/ is already normally allowed, this does not trigger _il_ to become _lu_.

## Verbs

Ferritumulic verbal paradigms are similar to those of Italian. However, notably, Ferritumulic actually has four standard verb declensions: -ari, -eri, -iri, and the newly developed -éri, sometimes also called -héri due to its restrictions.

Verbs in -éri have a stem that ends in a palatal consonant or an affricate, such as ᴄᴀᴘʜᴇ́ʀɪ, meaning "to take", whose stem ends in /pɸ/. In the indicative, they conjugate similarly to -iri verbs, but in the subjunctive, they conjugate like -eri verbs. The imperfect of -éri verbs is also identical to what it would be as an -eri verb.

Being a Pisco-Imerchali language, irregularity is fairly common, particularly when weighted by the frequency of the word. For example, _stari_ "to be" and _aberi_ "to have", two of the most common verbs in the language, are highly irregular, and even more regular verbs like _tenhéri_ "to hold" and _moriri_ "to die" have notable exceptions (the preterite forms for _tenhéri_ and both verbs' past participles).

## Nouns

Nouns have grammatical gender in Ferritumulic. Generally, this can be deduced from the ending of the word. For example, words ending in -u are likely to be masculine, and words ending in -a are likley to be feminine. Words ending in -i are variable, where some words, like vóʟᴘɪ "fox", can be either masculine or feminine, while others, like cᴀɴɪ "dog" are always masculine, with a unique feminine form like cᴀɴʜᴀ.

Nouns also decline for number and, notably for a modern Pisco-Imerchali language, case. There are three cases, nominative, accusative, and genitive. However, for nouns ending in -u or -a, the first two of these cases end up being identical in the singular. The accusative is also used for lative, ablative, and instrumental meanings using _ste_, _per_, and _cóm_ respectively, and the genitive is also used for the dative using _a_. Note though, that if the noun begins with a lateral consonant, _per_ becomes _pel_, and when adjacent to a non-labial nasal, stop, affricate or fricative, _cóm_ becomes _cón_.

For nouns with the same form in multiple cases, _di_ and _a_ can still be used to clarify genitive and accusative meanings respectively.

Some nouns, like sᴀʙᴇ, are always plural, while others, like ᴀcvᴀ, are always singular. Generally, nouns of either type are mass nouns like these two, but this is not necessarily always the case. These nouns _can_ have forms of the other type, in these cases ꜱᴀʙᴀ ᴀcvᴇ́, but these are not used in normal speech, and are largely relegated to poetry or other artistic uses of language.

#### Pronouns

Owing to their erosion, _autri_ (from "other") was stuck to the plural pronouns _nu, vu_, in line with many other Pisco-Imerchali languages, becoming _nautri_, _vautri_. The three forms of each pronoun can be used in the same way as with ordinary nouns.

## Adjectives

Adjectives' dictionary form, the masculine singular, often ends in -u, and this -u changes to -a in the feminine singular, -i in the masculine plural, and -e in the feminine plural. However, not all adjectives have unique declinations for all forms, and this is usually dependent on the masculine singular ending. Some adjectives end in -e or -é by default, and these change in the plural to -o and -ó respectively, and others end in -i by default, which generally means they don't decline.

Most of the time, adjectives go after the noun they apply to. However, if the adjective is considered an intrinsic property of the noun, either in general or for the specific object being referred to, it is often moved to before the noun. For example, a lengthy book would likely be _il libru longu_ since not all books are long, but if it's called an epic instead, it would be _la longa abenturóna_, since epics are necessarily long. Some sets can go in either location, such as _levi_ "clever" on _volpi_ "fox". Foxes are generally considered clever, so _il levi volpi_ is often preferred, but for a particularly clever fox, _il volpi levi_ might still be used instead.

Generally, only one adjective will be placed before the noun, and all but the last of adjectives in sequence will have -é or -i (depending on if the adjective already ends in -é or e) appended to the end. This affix does not affect stress, but instead of writing the old stress location with a grave, the interpunct, also used for other contractions, is used. A particularly clever brown fox will likely be referred to as _il volpi levi·é cautanhi_.

### Demonstratives

Ferritumulic has a three-level demonstrative system, albeit mutated from the Latin original, with _cwestu_ (from eccum iste) being used for items close to the speaker, _cwixu_ (from eccum ipse) being used for items close to the addressee, and _cwellu_ (from eccum ille) being used for items far from both the speaker and the addressee. All three decline as expected for an adjective whose dictionary form ends in -u.
