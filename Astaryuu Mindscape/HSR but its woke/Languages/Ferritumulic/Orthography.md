Ferritumulic still primarily writes using the Classical Latin script. However, it can be "modernized" fairly easily into the modern Latin script. There are some changes from Classical to Ferritumulic, mainly to accomodate phonological changes:

- Since /ks/ merged with /ts/, /ts/ always is written with ⟨x⟩, even when it comes from /tj tl/.
- Latinate /h/ and Amphorean and Greek /y/ are only consistently written in proper nouns, such as the placename Hyperborea (Ferritumulic writes this as ⟨Hʏᴘᴇʀʙᴏ̀ʀɪ̈ᴀ⟩). The latter in particular may still appear in other words, but not if they are of a certain age.
- [Amphorean](https://mc.nguh.org/wiki/Amphorean "Amphorean") gamma and eta were loaned for /x j/, partially since the last one is a mostly-loaned phoneme (old coda /s/ sometimes sounds like /x/, and so was rewritten at the same time). These are modernized into lowercase, fully-Latin script as ⟨kh h⟩. Kappa is also used for /k/ from Amphorean loans. In alphabetical ordering, eta and kappa take the place of h and k.

# Consonants
The cluster /βr/ is variably written as either of ⟨ʙʀ ᴠʀ⟩, though the former is more common.

# Vowels
Some words maintain the use of ⟨ʏ⟩, but this is still pronounced /i/ or /j/.

When used as an approximant, /i u/ use the second item in their cells; if intervocalic, /u̯/ is doubled (hence the ⟨w⟩). Additionally, if the stressed vowel is not the antepenultimate vowel, it is given the grave accent mark. Marking a vowel with acute and grave at the same time uses the circumflex.

Even though they now have semivowel letters, standard text is expected to write the sequences /i.V/ and /u.V/ with a diaresis over the vowel anyway.
