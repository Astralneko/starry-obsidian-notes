# Nouns
Nouns decline for the same cases as in [Rokadong](https://arcadianodyssey.miraheze.org/wiki/Rokadong "Rokadong"), although the forms of the particles are slightly different. Nouns are still in the direct case by default. Some of these changes resemble informal or dialectal variations of modern Rokadong, but Kairitelan had already done them by the time Rokadong started, so there seems to be no connection.

- Ergative _ká_, used for the agent of the verb
- Accusative _fá_, used for the patient (direct object) of the verb
- Dative _lak_, used for the indirect object
- Genitive _se_, used for descriptions, possessors, and origins
- Locative _ku_, used for the location of something

For some adpositions, _ku_ is often stacked onto them even if this does not happen in Rokadong. Since most such adpositions are monosyllabic, it's thought that this was done to strengthen those adpositions. Notably, this occurs in spoken Kairitelan, but old prose and most poetry does not do this.

_Ku_ also does not have an instrumental application in Kairitelan. Instead, the adposition _ere_, meaning "with", is used.

# Adjectives
Adjectives work slightly differently in Kairitelan versus its sister language Rokadong. They still come after the noun they modify, but Kairitelan has a dedicated negative affix for adjectives, _~(a)ken_. The _a_ is added if the adjective ends in a velar or stop consonant.

# Verbs
Verbs in Kairitelan usually either end in -r or -la, and conjugations are built on this assumption. As a result, loaned verbs that don't end in r, depending on their age, use different methods to fit into this framework:

- Really old loans use _la_, sometimes even if the source word ended in -r.
- Loans of medium age use _ar_ or _ir_ unless they end in a lateral, in which case _la_ is preferred.
- Recent loans use _yr_ unless they end in a lateral, in which case they use _la_ instead.

Unlike Rokadong, Kairitelan formerly-stative verbs, such as  _matyr_, are no longer considered verbs, and require a helping copula.

Verbs have a somewhat similar paradigm to in Rokadong. To negate a verb, it is surrounded with _re~ken_. Like the imperative and hypothetical, this replaces the -r or -la if present; therefore, the negative and negimperative are often considered moods. The copula, though, is different, as it does not need to be negated if one of its arguments (probably the patient) is a negative adjective.
