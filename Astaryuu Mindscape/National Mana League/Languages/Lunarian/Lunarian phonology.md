## Notes

- The Armenian letter fita <ֆ> is used in [asterIPA](https://arcadianodyssey.miraheze.org/wiki/AsterIPA "AsterIPA") to refer to a "whistle fricative", even though this [has an extIPA symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/s%CD%8E "w:s͎"); Astaryuu defends this by saying that Lunarian considers the whistle fricative a labiocoronal sibilant, while <s͎> is clearly alveolar.
- The uvular unvoiced fricative /χ/ is generally velarized [χᵚ], if not truly velar [x]. However, since it patterns with the uvular voiced fricative /ʁ/, it is considered uvular instead of velar.
- All approximants and trills (regardless of if they are lateral or not) become voiceless when before a voiceless plosive; [ɬ] just is the only voiceless approximant which is found outside of this context (though [ç] is often considered a voiceless /j/).
- Trills are unique in that despite being sonorous, they undergo purity mutation, likely because they're seen as similar enough to /d g/ to mutate. However, their mutation is very limited.
- The voiced fricatives /ʝ ʁ/ pattern like sonorants, in that they never undergo purity mutation, and may be replaced by an affixed plosive depending on which plosive is affixed. However, they still do not become voiceless when adjacent to a voiceless phoneme.

## Notes
- In Impure and Terrestrial Lunarian, nepo, dim, and esolc vowels become the equivalent open, mid, and close vowels respectively, but with their backness and rounding flipped. For example, the dim _front rounded_ vowel /ო/ becomes [ɤ], the mid _back unrounded_ vowel. This is also why the [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language "w:Georgian language") letters used for the imaginary vowels appear reversed compared to their actual values in Georgian.
- The vowels /e o ო ე/ are all true mid [e̞ o̞ ო̝ ე˔].
# Purity mutation
Purity mutation primarily occurs based on the purity class of the noun it refers to. It is mostly limited to Standard Lunarian, but Impure Lunarian does make use of first-level mutation.

First-level mutation applies to plosives exclusively, nuking them in contexts except before fricatives. Trills usually do not get mutated in first-level, but some dialects do lenite them in the coda or before a non-continuant (plosive or nasal).

Second-level mutation applies to all plosives, trills, and voiceless fricatives, but not to voiced fricatives, nasals, or approximants. Second-level mutation applies _after_ first-level mutation, such that for example, in the cluster /ata/, /t/ first mutates to [s] and then to [θ̞]. It is also more aggressive, destroying all remaining plosives.
