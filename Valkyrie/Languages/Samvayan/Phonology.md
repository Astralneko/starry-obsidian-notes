Samvayan phonology is somewhat similar to the real-life languages Sanskrit and Thai, with a set of basic consonants that can be mutated in various ways.
# Consonants
The consonant inventory of Samvayan has five places of articulation: labial, dental, retroflex, palatal, and velar. While there are *sensu stricto* only voiceless plosives, voiced plosives, voiceless fricatives, voiced nasals, and voiced approximants, *de facto* there are many mutations that can be applied to various sets of consonants.

|             | -   | Labial | Dental | Retroflex | Palatal   | Velar |
| ----------- | --- | ------ | ------ | --------- | --------- | ----- |
| Plosive     | -   | p b    | t d    | ʈ ɖ       | (t͡ɕ d͡ʑ) | k g   |
| Fricative   | -   | f      | s      | ʂ         | ɕ         | x     |
| Nasal       | -   | m      | n      | ɳ         | ɲ         | ŋ     |
| Approximant | -   | ʋ      | l      | ɻ         |           |       |
While this table could look neater, there are some serious concessions preventing it from doing so:
* The palatal approximant /j/ is heavily restricted.
	* The palatal approximant /j/ only appears epenthetically and intervocalically, and in both cases can be treated as the non-syllabic allophone of /i u/. Notably, this is not true of the other approximants, which can also begin a word.
* /w/ is marginally phonemic, as in most dialects, it has merged with a fricative to become /ʋ/.
* All sonorants can be syllabic.
	* Syllabic nasals are better described as syllabic approximants, as closure is incomplete for them.
	* These originate from lax vowels that have since been either nuked or merged with /e a o/.
	* Other notes can be found in the Vowels section.
* All approximants can be nasalized.
## History
Historically, nasalized approximants are the result of the sequences /NP/ rather than /NF/; while /NF/ *did* nasalize, those nasals have since died in modern Samvayan. This also means in related languages, nasalized approximants are reflexed as implosives, since this is what /NP/ turned into in those languages.
Voiced fricatives also previously existed in Samvayan, but have since generally merged with other sounds: β with w and sibilants with their unvoiced counterpart (or occasionally ɻ, in the case of ʐ ʑ).
Antique Central Valkyrian did in fact have /j/ as separate from /i/. However, initial /j/ was lost in favor of ioting the vowel á la Greek, and /Cj/ (where C is not palatal, as Antique Central Valkyrian did not have them) clusters were lost in favor of palatal C. Likewise, /Cw/ was lost in most dialects as a result of the merger with v; some dialects do retain it though, and it is both still written and still present in a lot of other-language loans.
Coda retroflex consonants did exist in Antique Central, but have since become palatal.
# Vowels
As stated above, all sonorants can be syllabic. Thus, the number of true vowels in Samvayan is surprisingly low.
```vowel-chart

add [ɪ] "(j)"
add [ʊ] "(ɰ)"
add [ʏ] "(ʋ)"
add [u]
add [e]
add [ɘ] "(l)"
add [o]
add [ɤ] "(ɻ)"
add [a]
```
Often, syllabic approximants are rendered, at least phonetically, using the equivalent vowel phones from the table above: /ʋ ɻ l/ \[ʏ ɤ˞ ɘ]; syllabic /j ɰ/ is identical to /i u/. Syllabic /l/ sometimes labializes to distinguish from /e/, but this often results in /o/ rather than /e/.
Before a nasal or nasalized approximant, vowels will both laxen and nasalize.
# Phonotactics
## Syllable structure
Samvayan has a syllable structure of CCVC. Codas can be any consonant other than retroflex occlusives and voiced plosives. Coda stops are also typically unreleased, as in Thai or Vietnamese.
Initials have to either consist of one of the following:
- A sibilant fricative followed by either a plosive or itself.
- A voiced plosive followed by either an approximant or its homorganic fricative.
- A voiceless plosive followed by an approximant, its homorganic fricative, or /x/.
If the approximant is /ɻ/, then the plosive can't be dental.
This is also why /t͜ɕ d͜ʑ/ are marginally phonemic, as they are best considered clusters, /tɕ dʑ/, instead. This is because although they pattern like stops and the unvoiced one can be aspirated by being followed by an /x/, approximants cannot follow them.
## Banned sequences
Many phoneme sequences are not permissible in Samvayan. Usually, the coda is destroyed if these come about as a result of affixation.
- ʋu is banned, even if the sequence originally came from Antique Central vu (as in that case it unvoiced)
- In most dialects, ʂ becomes ɕ before /i e/
- ɻo is banned, but in this case ɻa is usually the result
